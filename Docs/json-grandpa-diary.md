# 外公日记内容

## p01 First Day at the Plant

| markerId | 正文 |
| --- | --- |
| p01_body_01 | October 3, 1980<br><br>Five years of drilling blast holes underground with Tunneling Team 4. Today I was transferred to the coal washing plant at the foot of the mountain.<br><br>Twelve of us started at the plant today. Before we went in, they lined us up at the gate under a red banner for a photo.<br><br>I was given Room 207 in the workers' dormitory, second floor, the second door past the stairs. Four beds, one table, one stove. From the window I can see the cableway climbing the mountain.<br><br>A new job deserves a new diary. This is the first page. |
| p01_body_02 | October 14, 1980<br><br>Zhao Jianguo has the bed across from mine. Five years underground, like me. In the photo at the gate he stood right beside me.<br><br>On the night shift the old hands told us a story. Years ago, a worker slipped out by the north mountain road after his shift and climbed all the way to the ridge. The gate guard wrote him up. He said it was worth it. From up there you could see every light in the plant at once.<br><br>Jianguo has not stopped talking about it. Tonight he stood at the window a long time. I looked too. In the dark I could only make out the cableway, climbing toward the ridge. |

## p02 Room 207

| markerId | 正文 |
| --- | --- |
| p02_body_01 | November 9, 1980<br><br>Training is over. I am in the washing shop now. Jianguo was put on the cableway crew at the lower station, so our shifts no longer line up.<br><br>Tonight in 207 he brought up the ridge again. He has asked the old hands about it more than once.<br><br>So we made a deal. The first night we are both off, we go up to the ridge together and see the lights for ourselves.<br><br>He shook my hand on it, as if it were official. |
| p02_body_02 | December 4, 1980<br><br>Winter came early. We signed for heating coal at the coal yard and lit the stove in 207. Now the four of us sit around it before the night shift, gloves drying on the stovepipe.<br><br>Jianguo says the north road is no good in the dark. The empty buckets go back up the line all night, so we will ride one. Tonight he drew the way on the back of an old work ticket. Down the main road to the lower station, every tower up the line, and a cross at the top for the ridge.<br><br>He put it in the table drawer. He says it stays there until the night we go. |

## p03 Next Time

| markerId | 正文 |
| --- | --- |
| p03_body_01 | December 26, 1980<br><br>My girl is one year old today.<br><br>It was also the first night Jianguo and I were both off. Before I went home this morning, I told him I might not make it back by dark. He said go. The lights will still be there.<br><br>I did not make it back. She still will not let anyone but her mother hold her. My wife has not slept a full night in months. I could not leave her with all of it.<br><br>Next time, then. |
| p03_body_02 | July 18, 1983<br><br>I have not written in this book for a long time.<br><br>Jianguo and I were both off on Saturday night. We had it all planned this time. But it had rained for three days by then, and the water coming down the north gully washed out the footing of the fifth tower. The cableway was stopped.<br><br>They say it will run again after the rains.<br><br>Jianguo said once it runs, then. He took the map out of the drawer, looked at it a while, and put it back. |

## p04 The Cableway Stops

| markerId | 正文 |
| --- | --- |
| p04_body_01 | September 2, 1986<br><br>My girl started school yesterday. Her mother walked her there, with a satchel nearly as big as she is.<br><br>Jianguo is on nights now and I am on days. Most weeks we only see each other at the gate at the evening changeover, him going in, me coming out. Sometimes he says, the ridge. I say, next time we are both off.<br><br>The other two beds in 207 have been empty since spring. No new hands have come to the plant in a long time. |
| p04_body_02 | April 12, 1988<br><br>Word came this morning. The plant is stopping, and no one can say when it will start again.<br><br>At noon the cableway stopped. The buckets just hung there on the line, all the way up toward the ridge. Nobody in the washing shop said much. We cleaned the machines anyway.<br><br>Jianguo and I sat up late in 207 tonight.<br><br>From the window I can still see the cableway climbing the mountain. Nothing on it moves. |
